pH calculator

Convert between pH, pOH, [H⁺] and [OH⁻], or find the pH of a strong or weak acid or base. Every step is shown, with the log sig fig rule applied.

Count the H⁺ or OH⁻ in the formula. Ca(OH)₂ has two OH groups, so 0.050 M Ca(OH)₂ gives [OH⁻] = 2 × 0.050 = 0.10 M.
Strong acids: HCl, HBr, HI, HNO₃, HClO₄, H₂SO₄ (count 1 for its first H). Strong bases: NaOH, KOH, LiOH (1), and Ca(OH)₂, Sr(OH)₂, Ba(OH)₂ (2).

pH formulas (at 25 °C)

  • pH = −log[H⁺]   and   [H⁺] = 10−pH
  • pOH = −log[OH⁻]   and   [OH⁻] = 10−pOH
  • pH + pOH = 14.00   and   [H⁺][OH⁻] = Kw = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴

Sig figs for logs

The number of decimal places in a pH equals the number of sig figs in the concentration. [H⁺] = 3.2 × 10⁻⁴ M (2 sig figs) gives pH = 3.49 (2 decimal places). The digit before the decimal point only shows the power of ten.

Strong vs. weak

Strong acids and bases ionize completely, so [H⁺] (or [OH⁻]) equals the concentration times the number of H⁺ or OH⁻ per formula. Weak ones only partly ionize, so you use an ICE table with Ka or Kb:

Ka = x² ÷ (C − x)

If x is less than 5% of C, you can use the shortcut x ≈ √(Ka × C). This calculator always solves the full quadratic and tells you whether the shortcut would have been OK.
