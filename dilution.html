How the dilution formula works

When you dilute a solution, you add solvent but the amount of solute (moles) stays the same. Moles = concentration × volume, so:

C₁V₁ = C₂V₂

  • C₁, V₁: concentration and volume of the stock solution you take
  • C₂, V₂: concentration and total volume of the diluted solution

Rearrange for the unknown, for example V₁ = C₂V₂ ÷ C₁. Both concentrations must use the same unit, and both volumes must use the same unit.

Worked example

How much 2.00 M HCl do you need to make 250.0 mL of 0.500 M HCl?
V₁ = (0.500 M × 250.0 mL) ÷ 2.00 M = 62.5 mL.
Take 62.5 mL of the stock and add water until the total volume is 250.0 mL (about 187.5 mL of water).

Common mistakes

  • Using the volume of water added as V₂. V₂ is the final total volume.
  • Mixing units, like M with mM or L with mL, without converting.
  • Getting C₂ bigger than C₁. Diluting can only lower the concentration.
