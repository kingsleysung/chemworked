How to find an empirical formula

The empirical formula is the simplest whole-number ratio of atoms in a compound.

  1. Percent to grams: assume a 100 g sample, so 40.0% C becomes 40.0 g C.
  2. Grams to moles: divide each mass by the element's atomic mass.
  3. Divide by the smallest number of moles to get a ratio.
  4. Make whole numbers: if a ratio ends in about .5, multiply all by 2. About .33 or .67, multiply by 3. About .25 or .75, multiply by 4.

A handy rhyme: "percent to mass, mass to mole, divide by small, multiply 'til whole."

Molecular formula

The molecular formula is a whole-number multiple of the empirical formula. Divide the compound's molar mass by the empirical formula mass, then multiply every subscript by that number. Example: CH₂O (30.03 g/mol) with molar mass 180.16 g/mol gives 180.16 ÷ 30.03 = 6, so C₆H₁₂O₆ (glucose).
