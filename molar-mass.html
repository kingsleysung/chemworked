Molar mass calculator

Type a chemical formula to get its molar mass, the mass of each element, and the percent composition, with every step shown.

Capital letters matter: Co is cobalt, CO is carbon monoxide. For hydrates use a dot or *, like CuSO4*5H2O.

How to calculate molar mass

  1. Count the atoms of each element. A subscript applies to the element right before it. A subscript after brackets multiplies everything inside: in Ca(OH)₂ there are 2 O and 2 H.
  2. Look up each element's atomic mass on the periodic table (in g/mol).
  3. Multiply each atomic mass by the number of atoms.
  4. Add the results. The total is the molar mass in g/mol.

Worked example: water, H₂O

H: 2 × 1.008 = 2.016 g/mol
O: 1 × 15.999 = 15.999 g/mol
Molar mass = 2.016 + 15.999 = 18.015 g/mol (often rounded to 18.02 g/mol).

What about hydrates?

Add the water molecules too. For CuSO₄·5H₂O, add the mass of CuSO₄ plus 5 × the mass of H₂O. That gives 249.68 g/mol.

Converting grams to moles

Use n = m ÷ M: moles = mass in grams ÷ molar mass. To go from moles to grams, use m = n × M.

Atomic masses are IUPAC standard values. Your textbook's periodic table may round slightly differently, which can change the last decimal place.
